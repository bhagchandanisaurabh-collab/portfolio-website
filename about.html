About me

The world around us is constantly changing, and geospatial data gives us a way to observe, measure, and understand that change. From satellite imagery to detailed information about our cities and landscapes, these datasets reveal patterns that are often invisible at first glance. I work with spatial data to turn those patterns into clear insights, practical tools, and solutions that can support better planning and decision-making.

My interest in places and people started early. During my early days, I found myself especially drawn to geography and psychology, subjects that helped me understand how people interact with the spaces around them and why places develop the way they do. That curiosity led me to study Geography at Dyal Singh College, University of Delhi, and later to pursue a Master’s in Geo-Information Science and Earth Observation at ITC, University of Twente, where I specialised in Urban Planning and Management. Over time, my interest evolved from understanding places conceptually to analysing them through spatial data, Earth observation, and geospatial technologies.

During my internship at Geonovum, I had the opportunity to work with Dutch geospatial data and open standards, developing interactive tools around datasets such as BAG and CBS data. That experience strengthened my interest in building geospatial applications that are not only technically sound, but also useful, accessible, and connected to real planning and policy needs.

Outside of work and academics, sport has always been an important part of my life. I grew up playing cricket and taking part in athletics, and I still enjoy staying active and playing sports whenever I get the chance. I value the discipline, teamwork, and competitive spirit that sport brings, and it remains something I enjoy beyond my professional work.
